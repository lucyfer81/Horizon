This critique challenges optimistic narratives about AI&\#x27;s potential to autonomously drive scientific progress, highlighting a fundamental limitation in current LLM architectures. It matters for fields like AI-assisted research, automated reasoning, and the long-term goal of creating artificial general intelligence \(AGI\) capable of genuine discovery. The paper sparked high-quality debate with 162 comments, indicating strong community engagement on this philosophical and technical issue. The author later clarified that the paper is not claiming LLMs can &\#x27;never&\#x27; make discoveries, but is highlighting a specific, fundamental challenge regarding hypothesis generation.

**Background**: A scientific hypothesis is a falsifiable and measurable prediction that can be tested through experimentation. The scientific method relies on generating such novel, testable hypotheses as a key step for discovery. Large language models \(LLMs\) are AI systems trained on vast text data to predict and generate language, but their ability to engage in original, goal-directed reasoning akin to human scientific intuition is debated.
