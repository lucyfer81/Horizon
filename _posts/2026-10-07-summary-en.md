## [Francis Halzen wins 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for conceiving the IceCube Neutrino Observatory.](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Francis Halzen was awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for his decisive contributions to the conception and realization of the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. The prize specifically recognizes his role in creating this massive detector at the South Pole, which has enabled the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. This award highlights a monumental achievement in astrophysics, opening a new window to observe the universe using neutrinos, which are fundamental particles that can travel vast distances without being absorbed or deflected. The IceCube project has pioneered neutrino astronomy, allowing scientists to study cosmic phenomena like supernovae and active galactic nuclei in a completely new way. The IceCube Observatory is a cubic-kilometer-scale detector embedded deep in the Antarctic ice, using over 5,000 optical sensors to detect the faint Cherenkov radiation produced when a neutrino interacts with the ice. The project was completed in 2010 and underwent its first major upgrade in 2026, expanding its capabilities for detecting high-energy astrophysical neutrinos.

hackernews · solarist · Oct 6, 09:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49976265)

**Background**: Neutrinos are elementary subatomic particles with no electric charge and nearly zero mass, making them incredibly difficult to detect as they rarely interact with matter. To capture these &\#x27;ghost particles,&\#x27; detectors like IceCube use massive volumes of a transparent medium \(like ice or water\) to increase the chance of an interaction, which then produces detectable light via Cherenkov radiation. The Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station provides a unique, pristine ice environment necessary for such a large-scale observatory.

## [Gleam compiler now targets Erlang Abstract Form instead of source code.](https://gleam.run/news/gleam-doesnt-compile-to-erlang-source-anymore/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Gleam programming language&\#x27;s compiler has been changed to generate Erlang&\#x27;s Abstract Form directly, rather than producing Erlang source code as an intermediate step. This change was announced as a significant update to the compiler&\#x27;s architecture. This shift improves compilation speed and enables deeper, more seamless integration with the Erlang/OTP ecosystem and tooling. It represents a maturation of Gleam&\#x27;s compiler, aligning its compilation pipeline more closely with other BEAM languages like Elixir. The Erlang Abstract Form is an intermediate Abstract Syntax Tree \(AST\) representation used internally by the Erlang compiler; it is also the compilation target for Elixir. This change means Gleam no longer generates textual .erl files, which could simplify build processes and improve performance by skipping a parsing step.

hackernews · ingve · Oct 6, 08:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49975619)

**Background**: Gleam is a statically-typed, functional programming language designed for building reliable and concurrent systems. It primarily compiles to run on the BEAM virtual machine, the same runtime used by Erlang and Elixir, leveraging their fault-tolerant and distributed computing capabilities. Previously, Gleam&\#x27;s compiler worked by generating human-readable Erlang source code, which then needed to be compiled by the Erlang compiler to BEAM bytecode.
