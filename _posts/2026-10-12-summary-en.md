## [Critique Argues Passkeys Create Vendor Lock-In and Reduce User Control](https://nonfunctional.substack.com/p/passkeys-are-a-trap-do-not-use-them) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An article titled &\#x27;Passkeys are a trap&\#x27; argues that the authentication method, while marketed as a security improvement, introduces significant risks of vendor lock-in and reduces user control over their credentials. The author specifically highlights concerns about remote attestation and the potential for users to become dependent on specific platforms or devices. This critique matters because it challenges the widespread industry push towards passkeys by highlighting potential trade-offs between convenience and user autonomy. If the concerns are valid, widespread adoption could centralize authentication control with a few major tech vendors, making it harder for users to switch services or recover accounts independently. A key technical detail is that attestation, which can identify the make and model of an authenticator, is only requested during credential registration, not at login. Furthermore, browsers present a permission prompt when a website requests attestation, giving users a chance to deny it.

hackernews · laurex · Oct 11, 21:59 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50048312)

**Background**: Passkeys are a passwordless authentication method built on the WebAuthn and FIDO2 standards, using public-private key cryptography and biometrics for login. They are designed to be more secure and phishing-resistant than traditional passwords. Vendor lock-in refers to a situation where a customer becomes dependent on a particular vendor&\#x27;s products or services, facing high costs or difficulties when trying to switch to an alternative.

## [NYC&\#x27;s &\#x27;Click to Cancel&\#x27; rule for online subscriptions is now active.](https://www.nyc.gov/main/click-to-cancel) ⭐️ 7.0/10

New York City&\#x27;s new &\#x27;Click to Cancel&\#x27; consumer protection rule has officially gone into effect, mandating that businesses must allow customers to cancel online subscriptions through a mechanism that is at least as simple as the sign-up process. This regulation specifically targets recurring subscriptions for goods and services. This rule is significant as it directly combats &\#x27;dark patterns&\#x27; and manipulative design that make cancellation difficult, thereby empowering consumers and setting a precedent for digital consumer rights in the US. It impacts a wide range of software, media, and service subscription businesses operating in NYC, potentially influencing similar legislation elsewhere. The rule requires the cancellation mechanism to be &\#x27;easy to find&\#x27; and prohibits forcing customers to interact with a live representative if such interaction was not required for sign-up. However, it does not necessarily address all potential corporate loopholes, such as early termination fees for annual commitments, which remain a point of contention.

**Background**: This regulation is part of a growing global trend of consumer protection laws targeting &\#x27;negative option&\#x27; marketing, where a subscription renews automatically unless the consumer cancels. Similar &\#x27;easy cancellation&\#x27; requirements have been implemented in California and are proposed in the European Union&\#x27;s upcoming Digital Fairness Act. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission \(FTC\) also has rules around simple cancellation mechanisms for subscriptions.
