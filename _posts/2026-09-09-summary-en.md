## [Google DeepMind releases AlphaGenome Atlas, a high-resolution map of DNA variant effects.](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Google DeepMind has publicly released AlphaGenome Atlas, a predictive model and comprehensive map that estimates the functional impact of every possible single-nucleotide variant \(SNV\) across the human genome. The resource is now accessible via a web portal, providing predictions for thousands of molecular traits, including gene expression and chromatin accessibility. This represents a major leap in computational biology, offering a systematic, genome-wide view of variant effects that could dramatically accelerate genetic research and the interpretation of disease-causing mutations. It has the potential to revolutionize personalized medicine by helping researchers prioritize genetic variants for study and clinical diagnosis. The model can process DNA sequences up to 1 million base pairs long and simultaneously predict eleven different regulatory properties within a single architecture. While it covers non-coding regions, some community members have questioned its specific handling of promoter sequences and its practical utility for analyzing consumer genetic data like 23andMe results.

Predicting the functional impact of SNVs, especially in the vast non-coding regions of the genome, is a major challenge in genomics. Existing tools like CADD and FATHMM predict variant effects, but often focus on specific regions or traits. Projects like ENCODE have mapped functional elements across the genome, providing foundational data for such predictive models.

## [Mathematician Tristan Buckmaster claims a major Navier-Stokes breakthrough, sparking controversy over OpenAI&\#x27;s alleged use of his work.](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Mathematician Tristan Buckmaster and collaborator Levent Alpöge claim to have proven a significant, non-Millennium Prize Navier-Stokes problem related to finite-time blowup, which could pave the way toward solving the full Millennium problem. This claim is accompanied by serious allegations that OpenAI may have used insights from their work to train its own AI model, which subsequently produced a similar proof. This represents a potential major advance in one of the most important unsolved problems in mathematical physics, with implications for understanding fluid turbulence. The accompanying allegations of academic misconduct against a leading AI lab raise critical questions about research ethics, intellectual property, and the role of AI in foundational scientific discovery. The claimed proof is for a specific Navier-Stokes problem concerning finite-time blowup with smooth forcing, not the full $1 million Clay Millennium Prize problem, though it is a closely related and significant step. OpenAI has acknowledged it cannot rule out that de-identified user data, which could have included the mathematicians&\#x27; interactions, was used to improve its models.

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations are a set of partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluids and are fundamental to fields like aerodynamics and weather modeling. The &\#x27;Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem&\#x27; is one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems, asking whether smooth solutions always exist for the 3D equations or if they can &\#x27;blow up&\#x27; \(become infinite\) in finite time. Proving blowup would provide a counter-example, solving the problem in the negative and winning the prize. The controversy involves whether AI systems trained on user data can inadvertently absorb and reproduce novel mathematical insights from researchers using those systems.

## [Benchmark shows Qwen3.8 27B&\#x27;s 4-bit quantization holds up, 1-bit fails](https://quesma.com/blog/qwen38-27b-quantizations-benchmarked/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A benchmark analysis of the Qwen3.8 27B model found that performance is largely maintained down to 4-bit quantization, but collapses significantly at 1-bit. The analysis used Wilson 95% confidence intervals to account for run-to-run noise. This provides crucial, practical guidance for deploying the popular Qwen3.8 27B model on consumer-grade hardware with limited GPU memory, such as sub-16GB cards. It validates 4-bit quantization as a viable efficiency trade-off for real-world use, while highlighting the current limits of extreme compression. The benchmark indicates that only the 2-bit quantized version scores noticeably lower than higher-bit versions, with minimal difference down to 4-bit. A key community insight suggests that Qwen 3.8 27B may offset some quantization-induced quality loss by utilizing its &quot;thinking&quot; capability for longer reasoning chains.

hackernews · stared · Sep 8, 14:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49611128)

**Background**: Qwen3.8 27B is an open-weight, dense vision-language model from Alibaba, designed for tasks like coding and structured output. Quantization is a technique that reduces the numerical precision of a model&\#x27;s parameters \(e.g., from 32-bit floating-point to 4-bit integers\) to decrease its memory footprint and enable deployment on resource-constrained hardware. For Large Language Models \(LLMs\), 4-bit quantization is a common method to make models more accessible, while 1-bit represents an extreme form of compression with greater performance risks.

## [Kimi K3 \(2.8T\) runs at 1 token/s on a MacBook Pro via SSD streaming](https://github.com/argonautlabsai/deltafin) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Deltafin project demonstrated running the massive 2.8 trillion parameter Kimi K3 model on a consumer MacBook Pro, achieving an inference speed of 1 token per second by streaming model weights from four external Solid State Drives \(SSDs\). This approach bypasses the hardware&\#x27;s limited RAM capacity. This demonstrates a novel, hardware-agnostic pathway for running state-of-the-art, massive language models on consumer devices with limited memory, potentially democratizing access to cutting-edge AI. It highlights a significant shift from pure hardware scaling to innovative software and I/O optimization for model inference. The model uses the Kimi Delta Attention \(KDA\) and Attention Residuals architecture, activating only 16 out of 896 experts per token for efficiency. The 1 token/s speed is extremely slow for practical use, and the setup requires four SSDs connected in parallel to provide sufficient bandwidth for weight streaming.

hackernews · Argonautlabs · Sep 8, 20:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49616257)

**Background**: Kimi K3 is a 2.8 trillion parameter open-weight multimodal model from Moonshot AI, designed for complex tasks like repository-scale coding. Model weight streaming is a technique that loads portions of a model from secondary storage \(like SSDs\) into memory/VRAM only as needed for inference, enabling models larger than the available RAM to run, albeit with a performance trade-off due to storage latency.
