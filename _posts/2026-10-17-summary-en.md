## [Claude AI discovers NIST ECDSA curve seeds were generated using non-cryptographic lrand48\(\) and timestamps.](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude AI discovered that the seeds for the NIST P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521 elliptic curves were generated by the C library&\#x27;s \`lrand48\(\)\` function, which was seeded with a Unix timestamp, not a cryptographic hash like SHA-1 as previously assumed. This finding was made in under half an hour, resolving a decades-old mystery about the curve origins. This discovery is significant because it debunks long-standing conspiracy theories that the NSA might have embedded a cryptographic backdoor by manipulating the curve seeds, as the generation method was mundane and non-cryptographic. It validates the security of these widely-used curves in standards like TLS and digital certificates, potentially increasing trust in the cryptographic infrastructure. The \`lrand48\(\)\` function is not cryptographically secure, as it uses a linear congruential algorithm, and its state can be predictable if the seed is known. One seed, for the B-163 curve, remains unexplained, with speculation that it might have been generated similarly but contains a typographical error, preventing a match.

hackernews · ahlCVA · Oct 16, 18:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122800)

**Background**: ECDSA \(Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm\) is a widely used cryptographic standard for digital signatures, adopted by NIST in 2000. The security of ECDSA relies on the parameters of the elliptic curve, with NIST standardizing several curves \(like P-256\) whose seeds were previously believed to be derived from SHA-1 hashes of unknown inputs. The seeds are crucial as they define the curve&\#x27;s mathematical properties, and any weakness in their generation could compromise security.
