In a December 2025 blog post, software engineer Bryan Cantrill argues against using large language models \(LLMs\) to generate professional writing without disclosure, framing the practice as a breach of intellectual integrity akin to having one&\#x27;s &\#x27;fly open&\#x27;. He contends that LLMs are poor writers and, crucially, they are not the author, making undisclosed use deceptive. This critique addresses a fundamental ethical and cultural shift in professional practice as AI tools become ubiquitous. It challenges emerging norms in software engineering and other fields, arguing that authenticity and personal voice in communication are essential and that failing to disclose AI assistance undermines trust and intellectual honesty. The argument is not solely based on the current quality of LLM output but on the principle of authorship and authenticity; even if LLMs improve, undisclosed use may still be considered deceptive. The post has sparked significant debate, receiving over 300 comments, indicating its resonance within the tech community.

## [Asahi Linux Officially Announces Support for Apple&\#x27;s M3 Chip](https://asahilinux.org/2026/09/m2-episode-1/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Asahi Linux project has officially announced support for Apple&\#x27;s M3 chip, marking a major step in porting Linux to Apple Silicon. This achievement involves significant reverse-engineering and driver development work to enable the open-source operating system to run on Apple&\#x27;s latest hardware. This is significant because it provides users of modern Apple hardware with a viable, open-source alternative operating system, increasing hardware choice and longevity. It also represents a major technical triumph for the open-source community in reverse-engineering proprietary silicon without official documentation. The support is a result of extensive reverse-engineering, as Apple does not provide public documentation for its Silicon. However, community comments indicate that adoption blockers remain, such as incomplete sleep and HDMI support, and potentially suboptimal performance for specific workloads like AI inference compared to native macOS backends.

**Background**: Asahi Linux is a project that ports the Linux kernel and related software to Apple Silicon-powered Macs, started by Hector Martin. It does so by reverse-engineering the System-on-Chips \(SoCs\), which lack official, publicly available documentation from Apple. Apple Silicon refers to Apple&\#x27;s custom-designed series of ARM-based processors \(like the M1, M2, and M3\) used in its Mac computers.

## [Nitter and XCancel resume service after receiving legal advice](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter/commit/1428b4c2b4246f92a7e5b2673438e5fb39fcc4a3) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Nitter and XCancel projects, which are privacy-focused alternative frontends for X/Twitter, have resumed their services after the project maintainers sought and received legal advice. This decision follows a period of uncertainty regarding potential legal pressure from the platform. This matters because Nitter and similar tools are crucial for accessing public social media content without tracking, JavaScript, or ads, serving users who value privacy and open-web principles. Their continued operation represents a small victory for the ecosystem of independent, user-respecting alternatives against centralized platform control. Nitter is known for being significantly lighter and faster than the official Twitter site, and it operates by proxying all requests through a backend server to prevent user tracking. The projects were reportedly inspired by other alternative frontend projects like Invidious for YouTube.

hackernews · zImPatrick · Sep 6, 17:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49588988)

**Background**: Nitter is an open-source, self-hosted alternative frontend for X/Twitter built with the Nim language. It allows users to browse Twitter content without exposing their IP address or browser fingerprint to Twitter, as all interactions are handled through the Nitter instance. Similar projects like XCancel provide the same privacy-focused service, acting as proxies that strip away JavaScript, ads, and tracking from the original platform.
