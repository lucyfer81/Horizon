## [Garry Tan urges US open-weight AI labs to distill frontier models, challenging proprietary AI ethics.](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Y Combinator&\#x27;s CEO Garry Tan has publicly argued that U.S. open-weight AI labs should have the right to use model distillation techniques on proprietary frontier models, directly challenging the ethical and legal stance of companies like Anthropic and OpenAI. He contends that since these frontier labs trained their models on vast amounts of copyrighted data without explicit permission, they lack the moral high ground to restrict others from distilling their models. This position strikes at the heart of the debate over AI openness, access, and the sustainability of the frontier AI business model. If adopted, it could accelerate the proliferation of capable, cost-effective open-weight models, potentially democratizing advanced AI and undermining the competitive moat of expensive, proprietary frontier labs. Tan specifically frames the &\#x27;true AI doomer scenario&\#x27; as all frontier AI power being concentrated in a single proprietary provider. The argument hinges on the perceived hypocrisy of frontier labs restricting distillation after themselves training on scraped, often copyrighted, public data.

**Background**: &\#x27;Open-weight&\#x27; AI models refer to AI systems whose model weights \(the core learned parameters\) are publicly released, allowing for inspection, modification, and redistribution, in contrast to closed, proprietary models. Model distillation is a technique where a smaller, more efficient &\#x27;student&\#x27; model is trained to mimic the behavior of a larger, more capable &\#x27;teacher&\#x27; model. Frontier models are the most advanced, general-purpose AI systems developed by leading labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, often requiring immense computational resources and investment to create.
