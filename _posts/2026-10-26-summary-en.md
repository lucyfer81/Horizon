## [Oregon police demand $2.5 million for records on a single officer, setting a new barrier to transparency.](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/01/oregon-police-misconduct-public-records-cost-fee/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Eugene Police Department in Oregon has responded to a public records request from a local news outlet by claiming the search and processing would take over 44,800 hours of staff time and cost approximately $2.5 million. This fee is for records related to the conduct of a single police officer. This case exemplifies a growing tactic where exorbitant fees are used to effectively deny public access to information, undermining the core purpose of Freedom of Information laws designed to ensure government accountability. It has significant implications for journalism, police oversight, and the public&\#x27;s right to know about official conduct. The police department&\#x27;s estimate of 44,816 hours equates to roughly 21 years of continuous work for one person. While agencies can charge for the actual cost of fulfilling complex requests, critics argue this specific calculation is an absurd and deliberate obstruction, not a good-faith estimate.

hackernews · tdeck · Oct 25, 18:04 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50234232)

**Background**: The Freedom of Information Act \(FOIA\) is a U.S. federal law that gives the public the right to request access to records from federal agencies. Many states, including Oregon, have similar public records laws for state and local agencies. These laws generally require agencies to respond within a set timeframe \(often 20 business days for FOIA\) and allow them to charge reasonable fees for searching, reviewing, and duplicating records, though fee waivers are available for public interest requests. The process is intended to promote transparency but can be hindered by delays, excessive redactions, or high costs.

## [A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis.](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A research study found that a specialized convolutional neural network \(CNN\) model, based on the decade-old Inception-v3 architecture and fine-tuned on dermatology images, significantly outperformed frontier large language models \(LLMs\) on a skin cancer diagnosis task. The CNN was trained on over 68,000 images from the ISIC Archive and evaluated on a held-out test set of 200 lesions. This finding challenges the assumption that large, general-purpose LLMs are superior for all complex tasks, highlighting the enduring value of specialized, task-specific architectures in critical domains like medicine. It suggests that for high-stakes applications, investing in tailored models may yield more accurate and reliable results than relying on generalist AI. The CNN model was fine-tuned from a network pre-trained on everyday photographs, and the test lesions were rigorously excluded from the training and validation data to prevent data leakage. The comparison underscores that LLMs, despite their vast parameter counts and general capabilities, can be outperformed by smaller, older models that are expertly optimized for a specific, narrow domain.

hackernews · sungam · Oct 25, 17:15 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50233779)

**Background**: Convolutional Neural Networks \(CNNs\) are a class of deep neural networks particularly effective for analyzing visual imagery and have been a cornerstone of medical image analysis for years. Fine-tuning involves taking a pre-trained CNN \(often on a large dataset like ImageNet\) and further training it on a smaller, domain-specific dataset \(like medical images\) to adapt its learned features. Large Language Models \(LLMs\) are general-purpose models trained on vast text corpora, capable of a wide range of language tasks, but their application to vision tasks typically requires them to process image inputs through a separate vision encoder.

This highlights the ongoing struggle within the tech community over platform governance, balancing free expression with content moderation, and the role of corporations in controlling digital infrastructure. It matters because it seeks to mobilize developers and open-source advocates to build technical alternatives to centralized, corporate-controlled networks. The talk is scheduled for FOSDEM 2027, a major non-commercial, volunteer-organized conference for free and open-source software developers. This stance reflects a direct pedagogical challenge posed by generative AI in higher education.
