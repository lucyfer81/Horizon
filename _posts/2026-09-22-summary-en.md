## [Xiaomi releases MiMo v2.6 LLM with Flash and Pro variants, emphasizing transparency and affordability.](https://mimo.xiaomi.com/mimo-v2-6) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Xiaomi has released version 2.6 of its MiMo large language model, introducing two new variants: MiMo-Flash with 309B total parameters and MiMo-Pro with 1.02T total parameters. The release is notable for its detailed technical report and the public sharing of a real-time training dashboard. This release signifies a major Chinese tech company advancing the frontier of efficient, large-scale AI models, potentially increasing competition and accessibility in the global LLM market. The MiMo-Flash variant uses a Mixture-of-Experts \(MoE\) architecture, activating only 15B parameters per token for computational efficiency, while the Pro variant activates 42B. The models were trained using a novel, fully asynchronous Group Relative Policy Optimization \(GRPO\) method on very large batches.

In the LLM landscape, &\#x27;Pro&\#x27; variants typically prioritize capability and quality, while &\#x27;Flash&\#x27; variants are optimized for speed and cost-efficiency, a naming convention popularized by models like Google&\#x27;s Gemini.

## [TypeSafe AI Launches Jev, a &\#x27;System One&\#x27; LLM That Outputs Structured Decisions](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/21/jev/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On September 15, 2026, TypeSafe AI unveiled Jev, a new class of AI model they call a &\#x27;System One&\#x27; or &\#x27;Decision Model&\#x27;. Unlike standard LLMs that generate text, Jev takes text input and outputs structured probabilistic decisions—such as confidence scores, categorical choices, or numeric ratings—directly as floating-point numbers. This represents a significant architectural shift, moving LLMs from text generators to direct decision-making tools that can be integrated into software pipelines without parsing or post-processing. Its extremely low cost, charging only for input tokens, and high speed for parallel question evaluation could make advanced classification and ranking tasks more accessible and scalable for developers. Jev can answer three types of questions: Yes/No \(Noul\) questions returning a confidence probability, Choice questions providing a probability distribution over options, and Score questions outputting a numeric rating on a defined scale. A key caveat is its &\#x27;black box&\#x27; nature, as it provides only a numeric output without any explanation or justification for its decisions, raising concerns about bias and interpretability.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 21, 23:09

**Background**: Large Language Models \(LLMs\) like GPT-4 are typically designed to generate human-like text in response to prompts. In contrast, System One models are a new category proposed by TypeSafe AI, built specifically for fast, structured decision-making that software can consume directly. The term &\#x27;System One&\#x27; is inspired by Daniel Kahneman&\#x27;s dual-process theory, referring to fast, intuitive thinking, which aligns with the model&\#x27;s design for rapid, probabilistic outputs.
