## [Andrej Karpathy introduces &\#x27;Pelican,&\#x27; a new AI benchmark for 3D scene generation and physical world understanding.](https://twitter.com/karpathy/status/2083749667410727319) ⭐️ 8.0/10

AI researcher Andrej Karpathy introduced a novel benchmark named &\#x27;Pelican,&\#x27; which challenges AI models to generate an SVG image of a pelican riding a bicycle. This task is designed to evaluate a model&\#x27;s ability to understand and represent the physical world through 3D scene generation. This benchmark matters because it moves beyond traditional text-based evaluations to probe a model&\#x27;s spatial reasoning and physical commonsense, which are critical for developing AI that can interact with the real world. It highlights a growing focus in AI research on bridging the gap between language models and embodied, physical intelligence. The benchmark specifically asks models to generate an SVG, a vector graphics format, which requires understanding shapes, spatial relationships, and object interactions. Notably, some community members argue that success on this task may reflect a model&\#x27;s proficiency in writing specific code \(like three.js\) rather than genuine physical understanding.

**Background**: Traditional AI benchmarks like MMLU or GSM8K primarily test knowledge recall and text-based reasoning. In contrast, 3D scene generation involves creating coherent 3D environments from descriptions, a task at the intersection of generative AI and computer vision. &\#x27;Physical AI&\#x27; is a broader field aiming to enable AI systems to perceive and act in the real world, moving from digital bits to physical atoms.

## [Major Tech Companies Advocate for &\#x27;Open Weights&\#x27; AI in Response to Potential US Restrictions](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/2/open-letters/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On July 24th, Microsoft shepherded an open letter titled &\#x27;Open Weights and American AI Leadership,&\#x27; signed by 235 companies including NVIDIA, Amazon, and OpenAI, arguing against potential U.S. government restrictions on open-weight AI models. In response, Anthropic published its own position paper three days later, and a separate letter from over 1,300 frontier AI employees called for the U.S. government to support efforts to &\#x27;pace&\#x27; automated AI development. This debate represents a critical fault line in AI policy, pitting the benefits of open innovation, competition, and security through scrutiny against concerns about national security and catastrophic misuse. The outcome could determine whether advanced AI development remains concentrated among a few closed providers or becomes a more distributed, open ecosystem, significantly impacting global technological leadership and AI safety approaches. The Microsoft-led letter notably defends &\#x27;distillation&\#x27;—using one model&\#x27;s outputs to train another—as a legitimate development technique, countering arguments that it constitutes misappropriation. A key point of contention is the absence of Anthropic as a signatory, whose CEO emphasized risks from authoritarian governments and called for a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation, while still claiming not to advocate for an outright ban on open-weights models.

**Background**: In AI, &\#x27;model weights&\#x27; are the numerical parameters that constitute a neural network&\#x27;s learned &\#x27;knowledge,&\#x27; determining how it processes inputs to produce outputs. &\#x27;Open weights&\#x27; refers to models where these weights are publicly released, allowing anyone to run, study, and modify the model, which differs from &\#x27;open source&\#x27; that may include full training code and data. The debate arises amid concerns that the U.S. government, citing safety, might restrict access to powerful open models, potentially to prevent adversarial nations from gaining advanced capabilities.

## [Kakehashi: An experimental userspace project to run macOS binaries on Linux ARM](https://github.com/wie-project/kakehashi) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Developer vlad\_kalinkin has released Kakehashi, an experimental userspace compatibility layer that enables native execution of macOS command-line binaries on Linux ARM machines. The project currently has working prototypes for 7-Zip, curl, and Xcode Tools Git, with 7-Zip passing multi-threaded compression tests, albeit at a 5.2x performance penalty compared to native Linux. This project addresses a novel compatibility challenge, potentially unlocking a vast ecosystem of macOS command-line tools for the growing Linux-on-ARM server and development environments, such as those powered by Apple Silicon Macs or cloud ARM instances. It represents a significant step towards greater software interoperability and could reduce dependency on specific hardware platforms for certain workflows. The project is in an early experimental stage, with performance overhead being a current limitation, as evidenced by the 5.2x slowdown for 7-Zip. It focuses specifically on the ARM architecture and command-line binaries, differentiating it from broader projects like Darling, which aims for full macOS application compatibility on x86 Linux.

hackernews · vlad\_kalinkin · Aug 2, 16:26 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49145937)

**Background**: Running binaries across different operating systems typically requires compatibility layers that translate system calls and handle differences in the Application Binary Interface \(ABI\). macOS uses the Mach-O binary format and the Darwin kernel, which are distinct from Linux&\#x27;s ELF format and system calls. Existing projects like Darling provide a userspace compatibility layer for running Darwin/macOS binaries on Linux, but primarily for x86 architectures.
