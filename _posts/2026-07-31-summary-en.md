## [Physicists resolve muon magnetic moment discrepancy, challenging evidence for new physics.](https://www.quantamagazine.org/physicists-solve-a-muon-mystery-now-old-results-dont-add-up-20260729/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Physicists have resolved a long-standing discrepancy in measurements of the muon&\#x27;s magnetic moment, specifically its anomalous magnetic dipole moment \(g-2\). This resolution invalidates previous anomalous results that had hinted at physics beyond the Standard Model. This is significant because the muon g-2 anomaly was one of the strongest hints for new particles or forces beyond the Standard Model. The resolution means the Standard Model remains robust, redirecting the search for new physics to other areas and potentially impacting theories like supersymmetry. The resolution came from new, more precise experimental data from the Fermilab Muon g-2 experiment, which uses a 50-foot-diameter storage ring magnet originally from Brookhaven. The new measurement achieves a precision of 0.14 parts per million, strongly agreeing with the Brookhaven result but now aligning with updated theoretical predictions.

hackernews · ibobev · Jul 30, 15:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49111305)

**Background**: The muon is a fundamental particle similar to the electron but about 200 times heavier. Its &\#x27;anomalous magnetic dipole moment&\#x27; \(g-2\) is a precise measure of how it interacts with magnetic fields, serving as a sensitive test for the Standard Model of particle physics. The Standard Model is the highly successful theory describing known elementary particles and forces \(except gravity\), but it has known limitations, prompting searches for &\#x27;new physics&\#x27; beyond it. For over two decades, a discrepancy between experimental measurements and theoretical predictions of muon g-2 had been a major puzzle.
