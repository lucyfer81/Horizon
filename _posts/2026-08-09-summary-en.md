## [Tim Berners-Lee&\#x27;s 1998 essay &\#x27;Cool URIs Don&\#x27;t Change&\#x27; remains a foundational web principle.](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The 1998 essay by Tim Berners-Lee, the inventor of the World Wide Web, advocating for the creation of stable, permanent Uniform Resource Identifiers \(URIs\) has been accessible at the same URL \(https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI\) for over 28 years. This serves as a real-world example of its own principle. The principle of URI persistence is crucial for combating link rot, ensuring the long-term integrity of hyperlinks, and maintaining trust in digital information. It forms a foundational concept for the Semantic Web and digital preservation efforts, influencing modern web architecture and content management practices. The essay does not mention modern mitigation techniques like HTTP 301/302 redirects, which have become widespread due to SEO concerns and CMS features. Despite technological advances, the core challenge of preventing resource relocation or removal due to neglect or reorganization persists.

**Background**: A Uniform Resource Identifier \(URI\) is a string of characters used to identify a resource on the web, such as a web page. Link rot is the phenomenon where hyperlinks break over time because the target resource has been moved or deleted. The Semantic Web is an extension of the web that aims to make data machine-readable and interconnected, relying heavily on stable URIs.

## [A structured iterative method for using LLMs to master complex topics](https://laurentiugabriel.github.io/blog/articles/how-i-use-llms-to-learn/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A blog post details a practical, structured methodology where the author uses LLMs to generate layered explanations, diagrams, and quizzes on a complex topic, then iteratively refines and fact-checks the output. The process aims to create a personalized, interactive learning companion rather than relying on one-off queries. This approach addresses a key bottleneck in modern knowledge work—understanding complex systems—and demonstrates a shift from using LLMs for simple Q&amp;A to employing them as active, structured learning tools. It highlights the potential for AI to augment deep learning and knowledge management, a trend likely to grow as models improve. The method emphasizes iterative refinement and includes a fact-checking step, though the post notes this involves asking the AI to review its own work, which may not fully guarantee accuracy against hallucinations. It also leverages AI to generate visual diagrams \(e.g., via tools like Mermaid\) to aid comprehension.

hackernews · laurentiurad · Aug 9, 19:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49234675)

**Background**: Large Language Models \(LLMs\) like GPT-4 are AI systems trained on vast text data, capable of generating human-like text, explanations, and code. They are increasingly used beyond chatbots for tasks like code generation and summarization. However, they are prone to &\#x27;hallucinations,&\#x27; or generating plausible but incorrect information, making verification critical. Structured output generation and iterative reasoning are emerging techniques to improve LLM reliability for complex tasks.

## [Article Explores Pervasive AI Surveillance via Wearables and Potential Countermeasures](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/05/ai-wearable-surveillance-countermeasures/687203/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A recent article in The Atlantic examines the pervasive and often invisible nature of AI-driven surveillance, particularly through wearable technology, and discusses both its societal implications and emerging countermeasures. It highlights how corporations and governments are deploying these technologies, creating a continuous data-collection environment. This matters because the normalization of constant, AI-powered surveillance through personal devices poses a profound threat to individual privacy, autonomy, and democratic norms. It raises urgent questions about corporate power, government overreach, and the need for societal and regulatory pushback against the datafication of everyday life. The article references foundational research, such as the University of Chicago&\#x27;s &\#x27;Jammer&\#x27; project, which explored early counter-surveillance concepts. It also notes that despite widespread awareness of surveillance capitalism, public adoption of highly trackable devices like smartphones and wearables remains high, suggesting a complex relationship between outrage and complicity.

**Background**: AI-driven surveillance uses technologies like computer vision and data analytics to monitor behavior in real-time, often converging physical and cyber monitoring. Wearable technology refers to small electronic devices worn on the body that can collect sensitive biometric and location data. The concept of &\#x27;surveillance capitalism&\#x27; describes the business model of profiting from the large-scale collection and analysis of personal data for behavioral prediction and influence.
