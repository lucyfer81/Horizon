## [Fortran Celebrates 70 Years as a Foundational Programming Language](https://fortran-lang.org/news/2026/09-15-Fortran-birthday/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Fortran community is celebrating the 70th anniversary of the Fortran programming language, which was first developed by an IBM team led by John Backus in the 1950s. The milestone was marked by a post on the official Fortran-lang website in September 2026. This anniversary highlights the enduring legacy and continued relevance of one of the first high-level programming languages, which revolutionized scientific and numerical computing. Fortran&\#x27;s longevity demonstrates the critical role of stable, high-performance languages in maintaining and evolving legacy systems, particularly in fields like scientific simulation, aerospace, and finance. Despite its age, modern Fortran standards \(like Fortran 2003, 2008, 2018\) have evolved to support parallel programming and object-oriented features, making it competitive for high-performance computing. The language remains in active use, with some codebases dating back to the 1950s still in maintenance, showcasing both its stability and the challenge of technical debt.

hackernews · rbanffy · Oct 15, 15:03 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50104726)

**Background**: Fortran, short for &quot;Formula Translation,&quot; was created by IBM in the 1950s and is widely considered the first commercially successful high-level programming language. It was designed to allow scientists and engineers to write programs using mathematical notation, abstracting away the complexities of machine code and assembly language. Its introduction marked a pivotal shift from low-level, machine-dependent programming to more accessible, problem-focused coding, paving the way for later languages like COBOL and ALGOL.
